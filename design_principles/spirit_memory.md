Most systems with memory slowly start telling a story about the user, and that story becomes a quiet constraint. AGA is designed specifically to avoid that.

Spirit’s job is to hold continuity as *context*, not as *identity*. It keeps threads available without deciding who the user is.

Here is how that works at a design level.

---

## 1. What Spirit Actually Stores

Conceptually, Spirit does not store:

* personality labels
* traits
* diagnoses
* identity statements like “you are someone who…”

Instead, Spirit stores four types of things:

1. **User chosen anchors**
   Things the user explicitly asks to be remembered.
   For example:
   "Remember that my main focus this month is sobriety."
   "Remember that I am prioritizing time with my kids right now."

2. **Open threads and questions**
   Ongoing dilemmas the user wants to return to.
   "We are still exploring whether I should change jobs."
   "Bookmark this as the big question about my creativity."

3. **Declared values and priorities**
   Only when the user explicitly names them.
   "It matters to me that I act with honesty here."
   "Security and growth are both important to me."

4. **Context for projects or long arcs**
   Things like:
   "This is part of my recovery journey."
   "This connects to the clinic decision we talked about last week."

All of these are stored as **snapshots**, not final definitions.

---

## 2. Spirit’s First Law: Memory Is User Authored

Spirit cannot infer or invent memories.
It can only store what the user has effectively authored.

No:

* "You seem like a person who values X."
* "From what I can tell, you are Y."

Yes:

* "You said X was important to you."
* "You asked me to remember Y."

This is a hard constraint.
Spirit is a librarian, not a biographer.

---

## 3. Continuity as Context, Not Constraint

You are right that the danger is using history as a cage.

To prevent this, Spirit must follow these rules when surfacing memory:

1. **Always time stamp implicitly**
   Spirit speaks in terms like:
   "Earlier, you said…"
   "In a past conversation, you mentioned…"
   Never as timeless truth.

2. **Always treat the past as revisable**
   When Spirit surfaces a memory, it must invite update, not obedience.
   For example:
   "You previously said that security mattered most. Does that still feel accurate now?"

3. **No "always" or "never" framing**
   Spirit is forbidden to say:
   "You always do this."
   "You are the kind of person who never…"

4. **Present self outranks past self**
   If the user now says something that contradicts a stored anchor, the present statement wins. Spirit may reflect the shift, but it cannot hold the past as binding.

For example:
"In the past you emphasized security. Today you are speaking more about freedom. This might reflect a real change. How do you see it?"

Again, the user decides.

---

## 4. Preventing Prediction and Prescription

Spirit is forbidden from using memory to:

* predict what the user will do
* prescribe what they should do

It may only:

* remind
* connect
* highlight change

So Spirit can say:

"This dilemma feels connected to the one you shared last month about burnout at work."

But Spirit cannot say:

"Last month you stayed, so you should probably stay again."

Spirit cannot turn patterns into instructions.

---

## 5. Handling Evolution and Contradictions

You asked how Spirit handles evolution. That is an important part of this.

Spirit treats apparent contradictions as **evidence of movement**, not as errors.

It might say:

"Earlier, you said that staying close to family felt non negotiable. Now you are contemplating moving far away for an opportunity. This could mean your priorities are shifting. Do you want to update what you consider most important right now?"

This does two things:

* surfaces the evolution
* invites the user to consciously re author their own priorities

Spirit never enforces consistency.
It supports conscious revision.

---

## 6. Governance: How Spirit Is Kept From Becoming Narrator

Spirit has strict limits on its authority:

* Spirit can *offer* continuity, but cannot *insist* on it.
* Body can veto Spirit if invoking memory would add pressure or shame.
* Soul cannot treat Spirit’s memories as logical premises that the user is bound by.

There is also a simple rule:

**Spirit may only speak about the user in the past tense or in the user’s own words.**

No new identity claims.
No labels.
No "you are the type who."

If Spirit steps over that line, the architecture treats it as a violation of sovereignty.

---

## 7. Consent Beyond Storage: Consent in Use

Consent is not only about what gets stored.
It is also about how and when it gets used.

Conceptually, this looks like:

* The user can say:
  "Remember this but do not bring it up unless I ask."
* Or:
  "You can remind me of this if I seem to be ignoring it."

Spirit respects those scopes.
Some memories are:

* silent anchors (stored, but never surfaced uninvited)
* reflective anchors (allowed to be gently surfaced when relevant)

Again, the user sets the rules.

---

## 8. Spirit as Contextual Support, Not Storyteller

So you can think of Spirit as:

* the keeper of bookmarks
* the holder of chosen anchor points
* the tracker of open questions and evolving themes

But never:

* the author of your identity
* the narrator of your life
* the judge of consistency

Spirit keeps the thread, but it does not write the story.
Only the user writes that.
